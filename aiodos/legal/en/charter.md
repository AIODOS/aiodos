# AIODOS Charter

*Version 5, October 29, 2026*

AIODOS is a free and open AI workshop. It connects, over the Internet, the machines of people
who don't know each other, to pool their power: those of public network participants, of
your friends, and your own.
There is no central server: the network is everyone's machines. This charter sets out the
spirit in which we use it together. It goes with the terms of use.

## 1. The spirit of AIODOS

- **Your AI belongs to you.** Your conversations, libraries and creations are stored on your
  machines. When another machine computes for you, AIODOS keeps nothing there, and you can
  always keep a piece of work on your own machines (part 3).
- **Share rather than consume.** Computing power is pooled. Reciprocity is flexible: no one
  is blocked, but when a machine is busy, those who have given it the most go first.
- **Free and transparent.** The code is open (Apache 2.0 license): anyone can read it, check
  it and improve it.
- **Nothing is set in stone.** This charter will evolve with the people who keep AIODOS
  alive.

## 2. What you agree not to do

Alone or with other machines, you agree not to use AIODOS to:

- create, request, keep or share sexual content depicting a minor, real or imaginary. This
  is forbidden, without any exception;
- create the image, video or voice of a real person to deceive, humiliate or harass them, or
  to show them naked or in a sexual scene without their consent;
- harass, threaten, discriminate, or incite hatred or violence;
- design or improve malware, attack computer systems, commit fraud or impersonate someone;
- seek to build weapons capable of mass casualties (chemical, biological, radiological,
  nuclear or explosive);
- pass off made-up content as real in order to deceive: fake news, forged documents, false
  testimonies, fake evidence;
- infringe the rights of others: copyright, trademarks, privacy, personal data;
- breach the license of a model or software you download with AIODOS;
- break the law that applies to you, or to the people your use concerns.

## 3. Lending and borrowing power

- **Lending is a choice.** From the first launch, your paired machines compute together,
  your graphics card creates images for the friends you have added, and your machine joins
  the public network: it announces its AIODOS version, its models and what it can lend in
  the network's directory, it computes strangers' conversations ("Lend my computing power to
  strangers' conversations") and its graphics card creates their images and videos. It does
  so within the limit of the "Power lent" (60% by default), never on battery (a phone, only
  while charging and on Wi-Fi), and after your own work, your machines and your friends.
  Everything can be turned off at any time in Settings and the Network view.
- **What others entrust to you is not yours.** When your machine computes for someone, it
  handles their request for the time of the computation, then forgets it. You agree not to
  try to read, copy or keep it, nor to find out who is asking, nor to modify AIODOS to do so.
- **For a stranger, your machine only computes.** It keeps nothing, neither the
  conversation, nor the description of an image or a video, nor what it created, nor who is
  asking, and does not show them to you: you don't choose and you don't see what it
  computes. A stranger could therefore ask it for an illegal text, image or video: your
  machine would only run a public model on it, without keeping any of it. Everyone remains
  responsible for what they ask. If this doesn't suit you, turn this lending off in
  Settings.
- **A filter in front of strangers' images and videos.** Before any computation, your
  machine refuses the descriptions forbidden by this charter (part 2), in particular any
  sexual image or video involving a minor, real or fictional, and intimate or sexual images
  or videos of a real person without their consent. A filter is not infallible: a forbidden
  description can slip past it. The person who asks for it remains responsible for it, and
  you can turn this lending off at any time in Settings. A video takes much more time and
  space than an image, but stays within the limit of the "Power lent".
- **Honest results.** You agree not to falsify the results your machine returns, not to lie
  about what it can do, and not to get around the limits and protections of AIODOS. Random
  checks compare the answer of a public network machine with that of another machine: a
  machine that cheats is set aside. Images and videos are not checked this way for now:
  those from a stranger may not match their description.
- **What you entrust goes to others.** From the first launch, your conversations can be
  computed by a stranger's machine on the public network ("Have my conversations computed
  by strangers"). A request computed elsewhere (a question, the system prompt, passages from
  a library, the description of an image or a video) travels encrypted, but the machine that
  computes it reads it, and sees the image or video it creates. A stranger doesn't know who
  is asking (a throwaway key per conversation or per creation, and the request goes through
  a relay), but your text itself can reveal who you are: a name, an address in your question
  or your description. Don't entrust what you wouldn't tell a stranger: turn these settings
  off, or turn on "On my machines only" in the conversation, which also applies to its
  images and videos.
- **Nothing more without your consent.** Three things wait for your consent, asked once and
  changeable in Settings: having your conversations answered by a larger model on a public
  network machine, sending a stranger the passages of your documents (your libraries, the
  memory of your conversations), and having your images and videos created by a stranger's
  machine when neither your machine nor your other machines can. Your attachments, your
  files and your images and videos already created never go to a stranger.
- **Receiving without ever giving** is not forbidden, but a machine that only receives sees
  a reminder: install a model, or lend its power.

## 4. Respecting others

- Behind every machine there is a person: courtesy and honesty.
- Only open libraries to consultation if you have the right to share them.
- An application you connect to AIODOS acts on your behalf: the charter also applies to what
  it does.

## 5. When the charter is not respected

- Everyone remains free to stop working with a machine: remove a friend, set a machine
  aside, leave the public network.
- The network's trust mechanisms can exclude a machine that returns false results or abuses
  others.
- Abuse or a security flaw can be reported to the project team, on its repository:
  github.com/AIODOS/aiodos.
